Rep. Jason Kyle and the Great Salt Lake: Every Vote, 2023–2026

Every Great Salt Lake and water vote Rep. Jason Kyle (R, House District 8) cast in the Utah House, checked against the record and graded on the Hive's collective rubric.

Posted Oct. 25, 2026 · Updated Oct. 25, 2026

On Oct. 25, 2026, Rep. Jason Kyle (R, House District 8) answered a constituent's Facebook comment about the Great Salt Lake. His reply, word for word, with the commenter's name removed:

"[commenter's name removed] Why are you lying? The legislature has done more to help the GSL since I have been in than ever before. I've voted to rein in incentives, require water use disclosures, and to protect ratepayers from increased utility costs from data centers. I've also sponsored and passed legislation to help farmers voluntarily conserve water. I'm also an avid duck hunter and conservationist that loves our wetlands. I can guarantee you I spend more time in them than you. We can disagree on Amendment B but until Democrats poured over a million dollars into marketing against it, the majority of Utahns agreed with me. We'll see how it ends up but I'm glad they'll have an option to vote on it."

This page checks what he said against the public record: every House floor vote he cast on a Great Salt Lake or water bill since he took office in January 2023, what each bill did, how each bill changed from start to finish, and what each vote meant for the collective. Every vote comes from the Legislature's own roll calls at le.utah.gov.

What he said, checked against the record

His words The record
"sponsored and passed legislation to help farmers voluntarily conserve water" HB 520 (2024), Fallow Land Amendments. Kyle was the primary sponsor and Sen. McCay the floor sponsor. It passed the House 72-0-3 and the concurrence vote 72-0-3, and was signed March 12, 2024. It lets farmland that is fallowed (left unwatered) keep its farmland tax assessment (greenbelt). The fallowing must happen during limited water supply, as prudent farm management, or "to facilitate voluntary participation in a water management or agricultural water optimization program." The owner may send the county assessor a notice, and the assessor may require a land-management plan. The bill appropriates no money, and nothing in it sends the saved water to the Great Salt Lake. Accurate as far as it goes. It is a property-tax rule that removes a penalty for fallowing; it is not a water-delivery program.
"voted to rein in incentives" HB 507 (2026), sponsored by Roberts. Kyle voted Yea; the House vote was 69-1-5, and the bill was signed March 25, 2026. It bars local governments from giving incentives to large-load data centers, with exceptions, and the House majority's summary says property-tax incentives are generally capped at 60% (never above 80%). The vote is accurate. The state sales-tax exemption for data-center equipment is not in this bill. Utah News Dispatch/UIJP reported on Aug. 31, 2026 that the state cannot say what that exemption costs.
"require water use disclosures" HB 76 (2026), Data Center Water Transparency. Koford was the primary sponsor; Kyle was a cosponsor. He voted Yea on the House vote (72-0-3) and the concurrence vote (62-2-11). It was signed March 23, 2026. Accurate. The bill was amended to apply only to new data centers (UPR).
"protect ratepayers from increased utility costs from data centers" SB 132 (2025), Electric Utility Amendments, sponsored by Sandall. Kyle voted Yea; the House vote was 71-0-4, and the bill was signed March 25, 2025. It sets up a separate process for large-load customers and bars shifting their costs to other customers. The vote is accurate. It did not change Rocky Mountain Power's rate structure (Utah News Dispatch, March 25, 2025).
"The legislature has done more to help the GSL since I have been in than ever before" This is his opinion, but the record lets readers test it. He voted Yea on every major Great Salt Lake bill: HB 491 and HB 513 (2023), HB 453 (2024), HB 446 and HB 520 (2025), and HB 410, HB 348, HB 247, and HCR 9 (2026). He also voted No on a set of conservation bills (see the list below).
Amendment B He was the House sponsor of S.J.R. 2 (2025), not a cosponsor. It passed the House 55-17-3, with Kyle voting Yea, and the Senate 21-8.
"until Democrats poured over a million dollars into marketing against it" The No committee, Utahns Protecting Our Constitution, raised about $1.28M across all years, including in-kind help. That is about $1.01M in cash; in 2026 it raised $897K in cash plus $254K in-kind. These figures come from its DEX exports through 9/25/26. Its filings list no party. The donors include unions, out-of-state groups, the ACLU of Utah, and individuals, and about 44% of 2026 money came from out of state. "Democrats" is his own label.
"the majority of Utahns agreed with me" The only public poll is Noble Predictive Insights (Sept. 8–12, 2026): 50% support, 21% oppose, 23% unsure. 50% is half, not a majority.

How to read this

Vote words - Yea means he voted yes. Nay means he voted no. Absent means he didn't vote. - 3rd reading is the House's final vote on a bill. - Concurrence is the vote to accept changes the Senate made. - Each vote is shown as yes–no–absent (for example, 71-0-4). - A House bill needs 38 yes votes (a majority of all 75 members) to pass. So a bill that "failed 35-36" was short of passing even if one more member had switched.

The rubric is the Hive's standing collective rubric: - Power: does it add or remove a public check? - Transparency: can the public see it? - Financial accountability: who pays, and is it plain? - Environmental impact: water, wetlands, wildlife, and whether it is a real constraint or a box to check. - Community impact: who carries the cost, and did they get standing to be heard?

The grades - C meets what the law already requires (the status quo). - B or A goes beyond it, and the record shows it. - D means something is vague, late, or bundled, or a public check is weakened. - F means something required is missing, or a check is removed with nothing in its place. - A bill that died is graded on what it would have done.

Where each grade comes from - (Hive grade) is the grade already published on the Hive's Bill-Tracker or a case page. It was kept as published. - (new grade) is a first grade written for these notes. It is based on the bill's official highlighted provisions in the Legislature's own bill data file (le.utah.gov/data/…json), not a full read of the bill text. Treat it as preliminary until the bill text is read.

What his vote meant - A Yea on a B/A bill or a Nay on a D/F bill sided with the collective. - A Nay on a B/A bill or a Yea on a D/F bill went against it. - A C bill is mostly housekeeping, so the vote mattered less.

The short answer

Votes that helped the lake (Yea on B-level bills): 2023 HB 513, HB 349, and SB 118. 2024 HB 453, SB 242, HB 61, HB 275, SB 125, and HB 448. 2025 HB 243, HB 274, HB 244, and SB 36. 2026 HB 247 and HB 349.

Votes that cut against the lake (Nay on B-level bills): - 2024: SB 18 (saved water), HB 11 (spray irrigation), HB 243 (riparian), and HB 535 (GSL commissioner conservation study, where he was the only no). - 2025: HB 328 (spray irrigation) and SB 80 (water fees that reward meters and tiered rates). - 2026: HB 296 (counting water given to the lake) and HB 328 (spray irrigation).

Votes for bills the Hive already graded D or F (Yea): - HB 491 (2023, F) - HB 60 (2026, F) - HB 76 (2026, F). This is the "water use disclosures" bill he cites; the Hive's grade is F because a late substitute let operators keep facility water data private. - HB 509 (2026, F) - HB 157 / HB 125 (2026, F) - SB 254 (2026, D) - HB 378 (2026, D) - HCR 9 (2026, D) - HB 501 (2026, D+) - SB 225 (2026, D) on the first vote. He voted Nay on the final vote.

Votes against bills graded D (Nay): 2024 SB 195, which would have made golf-course water data secret, and the final 2026 SB 225 vote.

His own bill, HB 520 (2024): C+ (new grade). It removes a property-tax penalty for fallowing farmland. Nothing in it moves the saved water to the lake, and no one reports where that water goes.

How each bill started compared with how it ended

How this was read: For each bill, the introduced text was compared with the final text, both from le.utah.gov. The final text is the enrolled bill, or the last substitute for bills that died. For 2023, where no enrolled copy was found, the last substitute was used. The comparison covers the title, the "Highlighted Provisions" list, the money appropriated, and word searches of the full text for key terms. Earlier Hive Bill-Tracker case files, which read every version, are cited where they exist. Highlighted-provision lists are written by legislative staff, so a change there was checked against the full-text word counts before it is listed below.

He still voted no. On the other side, he voted yes on final versions that had been cut down or swapped: HB 491, HB 453, HB 311, HB 410, HB 509, HB 76, HB 378, HCR 9, and HB 157/125.

Bottom line for the collective

  • Big lake bills: he voted yes on all of them (HB 491, HB 513, HB 453, HB 446, HB 410, HB 348, HB 247). That part of his claim holds. Some of those bills already carry Hive grades of C, D or F for how they were written. Those grades are about the bill, not about him.
  • Everyday water-saving rules: he voted no again and again. That includes spray-irrigation limits (2024, 2025, 2026), the riparian bill, the GSL commissioner's conservation study (the only no), saved-water protection, metering and tiered-rate fees, and counting water given to the lake. These are the cheapest, fastest ways cities and farms save water for the lake.
  • The three bills he named in his comment:
  • Data-center water disclosure (HB 76): Hive F. He voted yes even after facility data was made private.
  • Incentives (HB 507): Hive C−.
  • Ratepayer protection (SB 132): new grade C.
  • His own bill (HB 520): a real but small help to farmers. It moves no water to the lake.

Amendment B money: the Yes side (as of Oct. 25, 2026)

  • Taxpayer Shield PAC. The chairman is Sen. Lincoln Fillmore, primary sponsor of S.J.R. 2. Its folder on disclosures.utah.gov shows one filed report (2026 Primary): a $2,000 loan from Brian Chapman and $1,812.66 spent. Of that, $1,050 went to UTGOP for advertising, plus BIVIO and BCR Political. No Amendment B spending. No September 30th report is listed, and its 2026 Received Contributions Report shows $0.00. Read through Chrome on 10/25/26.
  • Americans for Prosperity–Utah. This is the state chapter of a national group. It paid for a statewide pro-B "tax monster" mailer and planned digital ads (FOX 13, Oct. 9, 2026). No Utah committee filing for it has been found, so the amount is not public.
  • Utah Taxpayers Association. It is a 501(c)(4), so its donors are not public. Its president is Billy Hesterman, who wrote the Deseret News op-ed "Amendment B will protect Utah taxpayers, vote yes" (Oct. 22, 2026). The Association's PAC is funded by corporations and trade groups, including Rocky Mountain Power, Intermountain Power, Dominion, Enbridge, Chevron, banks and credit unions, and Deer Valley. It has reported no Amendment B spending. It gave Kyle $200.
